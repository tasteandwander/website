Roman food was born in the street, the slaughterhouse, the market stall. The flavour routes of Rome pass through its quartieri popolari: Trastevere and Testaccio are the heartland, but Garbatella, San Lorenzo, and Ostiense are full of historic osterie where the smells of slow-cooked food still mix with the ghost of the city's working past — cobblers, charcoal sellers, market women who kept the old recipes alive.

The cornerstone of this tradition is the quinto quarto — the "fifth quarter": offal, tripe, tail, kidney, heart, lung. What the aristocracy discarded, the people of Rome turned into something extraordinary. From that same humility came the pasta dishes now known across the world: carbonara, amatriciana, gricia, cacio e pepe — all built on a handful of local ingredients, nothing imported, nothing wasted.

Below are the six neighbourhoods that matter most if you want to eat well. Pick yours, or let the map find you.

The golden rules of eating in Rome

Rule 01 — Carbonara
No cream. Never cream. Guanciale, eggs, Pecorino, black pepper.
Rule 02 — Pizza
Roman pizza is thin and crisp. Thick dough is Neapolitan — beautiful, but calling it Roman is an affront.
Rule 03 — Coffee
You stand at the bar. Three sips. No cappuccino after 11am if you want to be taken seriously.
Rule 04 — The Bill
Coperto is normal and legal. Bread brought without asking means you're paying for it. No complaints.

The offal question

Quinto Quarto
The "fifth quarter" — offal, intestines, tail, tripe. This was slaughterhouse workers' wages. It became Testaccio's identity. Try one dish. Start with trippa.
Start here
Trippa alla Romana: tripe in tomato with mint and Pecorino. Rich and bright, not confrontational. The gentlest entry into offal Rome.
The brave move
Rigatoni alla Pajata — pasta with veal intestines still containing the mother's milk. Earthy, intense, entirely Roman. Available in very few places now.

The seasonal rule

Artichoke season
Carciofi alla Giudia is at its best February–May, when the Romanesco artichoke is in season. Out of season, politely decline.
Shabbat timing
Many restaurants close Friday evening through Saturday. Some of the best spots won't be open Saturday lunch — plan accordingly.
Kosher rules
Many Ghetto restaurants are kosher — no mixing of meat and dairy. Don't be surprised when Pecorino doesn't come with the meat dishes.

The Monti approach

Aperitivo hour
Monti has the best aperitivo scene in Rome. From 6–8pm, small enotece serve snacks with natural wine at prices that make the rest of the city look embarrassed.
Avoid the main square
Piazza della Madonna dei Monti is beautiful but its restaurants are tourist-facing. Walk one block in any direction to find the real neighbourhood.
