Appetizers

The Roman table opens with fried things. Salt cod, rice balls, zucchini flowers — battered, golden, eaten standing. The best appetizer in Rome costs less than a coffee and disappears in two bites.

3dishes
Supplì al Telefono
Street food Year-round

Golden-fried rice balls filled with rich ragù or vibrant tomato sauce, and a core of mozzarella that stretches with every bite — like a telephone cord, hence the name.

RiceMozzarellaRagùEggsBreadcrumbsOlive oil
Where to taste Supplì Roma, Via di S. Francesco a Ripa 137, Trastevere — no sign, just the queue
Fiori di Zucca
Jewish Ghetto Spring · Summer

Zucchini blossoms gently stuffed with mozzarella and a whisper of anchovy, dipped in a gossamer batter and fried until just crisp. Born in the Ghetto, eaten everywhere.

Zucchini flowersMozzarellaAnchovyFlourSparkling water
Where to taste Jewish Quarter (Ghetto) trattorias — Nonna Betta is the benchmark
Baccalà Fritto alla Romana
Friggitoria Year-round

Salt cod wrapped in a light, barely-there batter and fried to a pale gold. A dish from the Jewish-Roman kitchen that escaped the Ghetto and conquered the whole city.

Salt codFlourWaterOlive oil
Where to taste Testaccio and Jewish Ghetto — look for friggitorie, not restaurants
Primi piatti

First Course

Four ingredients, infinite arguments. The Roman pasta canon — carbonara, amatriciana, gricia, cacio e pepe — is built on guanciale, Pecorino, black pepper, and pasta water. Nothing imported, nothing hidden, no cream.

6dishes
Carbonara
Roman icon No cream · Ever

Pasta entwined with a luscious, velvety sauce of eggs and Pecorino, studded with crispy guanciale. The most debated dish in Italian gastronomy — Rome holds the only correct answer.

Spaghetti / RigatoniGuancialeEggsPecorino RomanoBlack pepper
Where to taste Tonnarello (Trastevere) · Nannarella (Trastevere) · Felice a Testaccio
Amatriciana
Pasta From Amatrice

Pasta bathed in tomato sauce slowly simmered to deepen its flavor, enriched by crispy guanciale. Gricia's older sibling, once the tomato arrived from the New World.

Bucatini / RigatoniGuancialeTomatoPecorinoChili flakes
Where to taste Ristorante Roscioli · Campo de' Fiori area trattorias
Cacio e Pepe
Pasta 3 ingredients only

Pasta wrapped in a creamy Pecorino emulsion with freshly cracked pepper. Three ingredients — pasta, cheese, pepper — that require more technique than anything with twenty.

TonnarelliPecorino RomanoBlack pepperPasta water
Where to taste Felice a Testaccio — prepared tableside
Gricia
Pasta The oldest of four

Pasta tossed with crisp guanciale and a generous snowfall of Pecorino. The ancestor of both carbonara and amatriciana — pre-tomato, pre-egg, purely Roman.

TonnarelliGuancialePecorinoBlack pepper
Where to taste Sellero & Ventresca · traditional trattorias
Gnocchi alla Romana
Semolina Thursday dish

Soft semolina rounds baked until their edges turn golden, layered with butter and Parmigiano. Not potato gnocchi — something older, heavier, and more comforting.

SemolinaButterEgg yolkMilkParmigianoNutmeg
Where to taste Classic Roman trattorias in Campo de' Fiori area
Rigatoni alla Pajata
Quinto quarto Rare · Testaccio

Rigatoni in a rich tomato sauce infused with veal intestines still containing the mother's milk — pajata. One of the most Roman dishes that exists. Available in very few places now.

RigatoniPajata (veal intestine)TomatoOnionWhite wine
Where to taste Testaccio trattorias — ask before expecting it on the menu
Secondi piatti · Il quinto quarto

Second Course

This is where Rome's true character shows. The slaughterhouse workers of Testaccio were paid partly in offal — the parts the rich refused. They turned them into something extraordinary. The fifth quarter is the first course of Roman identity.

6dishes
Coda alla Vaccinara
Slow-braised Winter · Sundays

Slow-cooked oxtail melting into silky strands, in a tomato sauce with celery, cocoa, and pine nuts that gently simmers it into something lush, comforting, and quietly irresistible.

OxtailTomatoCeleryCarrotCocoaPine nutsWhite wine
Where to taste Tonnarello · Nannarella · traditional Trastevere trattorias
Saltimbocca alla Romana
Veal "Jumps in the mouth"

Thin veal kissed with sage and covered in prosciutto, gently sautéed until the edges turn golden and the flavors fuse into a delicate, savory whisper. The name says everything.

VealProsciutto crudoSageButterWhite wine
Where to taste Traditional Roman trattorias — Prati has reliable versions
Trippa alla Romana
Quinto quarto Saturday dish

Tender ribbons of slow-cooked tripe simmered in fragrant tomato, brightened with mint and crowned with Pecorino. The gentlest entry into offal Rome — rich, bright, not confrontational.

TripeSan Marzano tomatoPecorino RomanoMintWhite wineCelery
Where to taste Felice a Testaccio · Sellero & Ventresca · Testaccio trattorias
Fagioli con le Cotiche
Peasant dish Winter · Monti

Tender borlotti beans and strips of pork rind simmered in a rich savory sauce that clings to every bite. Warming, ancient, entirely Roman — the definition of cucina povera done right.

Borlotti beansPork rindTomatoOnionCeleryGarlic
Where to taste Monti trattorias · traditional Trastevere osterie
Carciofi alla Romana
Vegetable Feb – May only

Artichokes gently opened like quiet spring blossoms, filled with mint, parsley, and garlic, then braised in wine and olive oil until yielding and deeply aromatic.

Romanesco artichokeMintParsleyGarlicWhite wineOlive oil
Where to taste Any Roman trattoria in season — insist on Romanesco variety
Carciofi alla Giudia
Jewish Ghetto Feb – May · Since 1555

Artichoke deep-fried whole until the petals crisp like chips while the heart stays tender. Just salt — no batter, no gilding. Born in the Ghetto, adopted by all of Rome.

Romanesco artichokeOlive oilSalt
Where to taste Nonna Betta, Via del Portico d'Ottavia 16 · Jewish Ghetto
Pane e impasti

Doughs & Breads

Roman dough is thin, honest, and fiercely local. Pizza al taglio sold by weight at the counter, crisp Roman-style rounds baked in wood-fired ovens, and the ciriola — the city's own bread roll with a crackle outside and tenderness within.

3dishes
Pizza al Taglio
Sold by weight Year-round

Slices of airy, long-fermented dough baked in rectangular pans, toppings like edible mosaics. Eaten standing at the counter. The democratic lunch of Rome.

FlourBeer yeastWaterSaltOlive oil
Where to taste Antico Forno Roscioli, Via dei Chiavari 34 — go before noon
Pizza Romana
Thin & crisp Not Neapolitan

Thin, crisp, a sheet of golden dough — a canvas for generous toppings. The Roman answer to Naples: less air, more crunch, eaten flat without the fold. An affront to compare them.

FlourBeer yeastWaterSalt
Where to taste Roman pizzerie al taglio and wood-fired forno throughout the city
Ciriola
Bread roll Roman morning

Rome's own bread roll — airy with a gentle crackle outside and a soft, yielding heart. The vessel of choice for a porchetta sandwich or simply eaten warm from the forno.

FlourSourdough starterWaterSalt
Where to taste Any forno in the city from 7am — also available at supermarkets
Dolci romani

Desserts

Roman desserts are not elaborate. A bun split open and filled with cream. A crunchy wine ring. A pastry holding ricotta and sour cherries. Simple, ancient, precise. The kind of sweetness that works best at a bar counter at 8am.

3dishes
Maritozzo con Panna
Breakfast pastry Before 10am

A soft, slightly sweet bun split open and filled with an unreasonable amount of fresh whipped cream. The Roman breakfast — eaten standing at a bar, coffee in the other hand.

FlourEggsMilkButterSugarOrange zestWhipped cream
Where to taste Regoli Pasticceria, Via dello Statuto 60 — go early, they sell out
Ciambelline al Vino
Biscuit Castelli Romani

Crunchy, fragrant rings kissed with red or white wine, sugar, and a whisper of anise. From the Castelli Romani hills outside the city — ancient, humble, addictive with a glass of the same wine used to make them.

FlourSugarOilRed or white wineAnise seeds
Where to taste Bakeries and forno throughout Rome · osterie in the Castelli Romani
Crostata Ricotta e Visciole
Jewish Ghetto Year-round

Delicate shortcrust pastry holding a duet of creamy sheep's ricotta and tangy sour cherry jam. A Jewish-Roman dessert of remarkable subtlety — the ricotta is hidden inside, revealed only when you cut it.

FlourButterSugarSheep ricottaSour cherry jamEggs
Where to taste Regoli Pasticceria · Il Forno del Ghetto · Antico Forno Roscioli
